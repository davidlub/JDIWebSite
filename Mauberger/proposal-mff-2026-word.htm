Letter from the Chair

Letter of Introduction

June 2026

The Trustees
Mauerberger Foundation Fund
Cape Town

Dear Trustees,

The Jewish Democratic Initiative was founded nine years ago on a simple but demanding premise: that South African Jews have a responsibility to speak out when injustice is perpetrated in the name of the Jewish people — whether in Gaza, the West Bank, or here in our own community.

In those nine years, we have built something that did not previously exist. We are an independent, volunteer-run organisation providing a home for Jews who believe in social justice, democratic pluralism, and a negotiated settlement in Israel-Palestine. Our members include lawyers, academics, artists, doctors and community leaders. Our founding chairs, Anton Harber and Irwin Manoim, helped build the Weekly Mail — the newspaper that kept democratic journalism alive through the apartheid years.

This year's Festival of Dangerous Ideas — our second — takes place on Saturday 18 and Sunday 19 July, at the Hellenic Community Centre in Cape Town and the Killarney Country Club in Johannesburg. It falls on Nelson Mandela's birthday: a deliberate choice. South Africa's own journey from apartheid to negotiated democracy is the model we hold up for Israel-Palestine. Mandela's birthday is the right occasion to say so, loudly and clearly.

The speaker line-up is extraordinary. Avrum Burg, former Speaker of the Knesset and interim President of Israel, will be with us in person across both days. Arab Barghouti — son of Marwan Barghouti, widely described as the Palestinian Mandela — will meet Azhar Cachalia, a founding member of the Free Mandela Campaign, in what may be the most resonant conversation in recent South African civil society history. They are joined by Sally Abed of Standing Together, Rabbi Arik Ascherman, Molly Crabapple, Jonathan Shapiro (Zapiro), Mo Husseini, Samer Sinijlawi, and others.

We are grateful to the Mauerberger Foundation Fund for its previous support, and for the courage it showed in maintaining that support when it came under sustained attack from within the Jewish community. We spoke out publicly in the Foundation's defence when that happened — not strategically, but because it was true and it was right. The relationship between our organisations is grounded in shared values, and we are proud of that.

We present this proposal with confidence. The work is urgent, the speakers are booked, the venues are confirmed. What we need is your partnership.

Irwin Manoim

Chair, Jewish Democratic Initiative SA   |   info@jdisa.org


Executive Summary

JDI SA in 2026: An Independent Voice at a Critical Moment

The Jewish Democratic Initiative is South Africa's only independent progressive Jewish organisation — a non-profit founded by community members who believe that the Jewish prophetic tradition demands we speak out for justice, not merely for Jews.

As the conflict in Gaza enters its third year, the divisions within Jewish communities worldwide have deepened. South Africa's Jewish community is no exception. The mainstream communal bodies have, in our assessment, failed to provide moral leadership: they have closed ranks around Israeli government policy, silenced dissent, and excluded those who hold different views. JDI exists to fill that void.

Key facts at a glance:

  • 9 years of independent advocacy, founded June 2017
  • 30+ public statements published since October 7th 2023
  • 20+ events and webinars held in the past 18 months
  • 2 annual Festivals of Dangerous Ideas — 2025 and 2026
  • Daily news service (Chadashot) delivered free via WhatsApp

About the Organisation

Who We Are and What We Stand For

JDI is an umbrella organisation, not a monolith. Our members hold a range of views about Israel-Palestine but are united in one core belief: that justice requires an honest, equitable, negotiated settlement in which all parties — Israeli and Palestinian — are represented.

We were founded in June 2017, the 50th anniversary of the Israeli occupation of the West Bank and Gaza, by a group of South African Jews who felt that the mainstream communal bodies no longer represented their values. Our founding was inspired by South Africa's own tradition of Jewish activism — the disproportionate Jewish contribution to the anti-apartheid struggle — and by the recognition that silence in the face of injustice is itself a choice.

JDI operates on a fully volunteer basis. All directors are unpaid. We are funded through member donations, event income, and the support of foundations like the Mauerberger Foundation Fund.

What JDI Does

Public Statements

We publish statements on key events — from Israeli policy decisions to South African politics — providing a Jewish progressive perspective in the public record. Over 30 statements have been published since October 7th 2023.

Education & Events

Webinars, panel discussions, film screenings, and our annual Festival of Dangerous Ideas — bringing voices to South African audiences that are rarely heard in mainstream communal spaces.

Chadashot — Daily News Service

A free daily WhatsApp news digest covering Israel-Palestine and South African Jewry. Sent to hundreds of subscribers, curated by our editorial team. A unique and valued resource in our community.

International Solidarity

JDI is a member of JLink International — a network of like-minded Jewish progressive organisations across the diaspora. This network amplifies our advocacy and connects us with leading thinkers in Israel-Palestine dialogue globally.

Centrepiece of our 2026 Programme

The Festival of Dangerous Ideas 2026

Our second Festival brings together the voices you will not hear at your shul, your dinner table, or your corporate gala — over two extraordinary days, simultaneously in Cape Town and Johannesburg.

The Festival takes place on Saturday 18 and Sunday 19 July — Nelson Mandela's birthday. This is a deliberate symbolic choice. JDI's core contention is that South Africa's own negotiated settlement — achieved against overwhelming odds through dialogue between sworn enemies — offers a genuine model for Israel-Palestine. We have chosen speakers who are neither propagandists nor comfortable, people who have paid a real price for their convictions and who refuse the scripts on offer from either side.

The underlying theme: How do Jews and Palestinians break free of the spiral of violence and find peace?

The Speakers

Saturday 14:15 & Sunday Closing

Avrum Burg

Conscience of Israel — In Person

Once one of Israel's most influential political figures — former Speaker of the Knesset, interim President, chairman of the Jewish Agency — Avrum Burg has spent the last two decades as his country's most searching critic. He has called for Jews worldwide to petition the International Court of Justice over crimes against humanity in Gaza. His presence in South Africa on Mandela's birthday is itself a statement.

Saturday 15:30

Arab Barghouti & Azhar Cachalia

Free Barghouti Campaign / Free Mandela Campaign — In Person, Johannesburg

Arab Barghouti — son of Marwan Barghouti, the imprisoned Palestinian leader widely described as "the Palestinian Mandela" — in dialogue with Azhar Cachalia, a founding member of South Africa's Free Mandela Campaign and retired judge on the Supreme Court of Appeal. Two campaigns, two freedom fighters, one conversation about what liberation actually requires.

Sunday 10:00

Sally Abed

Co-Director, Standing Together — Via Zoom

Co-director of Standing Together — Israel's largest grassroots movement of Jews and Arabs — which recently formed a political party contesting elections on a platform of partnership rather than division. Abed sits on the Haifa City Council. A Palestinian woman who insists that liberation and equality for all who live on the land are not a compromise, but the only way forward.

Sunday 11:30

Rabbi Arik Ascherman

Rabbi for Human Rights — Via Zoom

For three decades, Ascherman has practised "protective presence" activism: physically shielding Palestinian farmers from settler violence. He has been stabbed, beaten, and arrested for this work. A recipient of the Gandhi Peace Prize, he insists that Israeli safety and Palestinian rights are not competing demands — they are the same struggle.

Sunday 09:00

Jonathan Shapiro (Zapiro)

South Africa's Most Celebrated Cartoonist — In Person, Cape Town

For three decades the country's most admired political cartoonist. Zapiro will speak about the silencing of images: how Israel-Palestine has become a "no-go" area for cartoonists at leading newspapers worldwide, as pro-Israel lobbying intimidates editors into censorship or dismissal.

Saturday 20:00

Mo Husseini

Palestinian Filmmaker & Activist — Via Zoom

A Palestinian-American who studied at UC Berkeley and worked at George Lucas's Industrial Light & Magic. His viral "50 Completely True Things" reached hundreds of thousands across the political spectrum. He co-directed The Path Forward alongside Oscar-nominated Julie Cohen. One of the most distinctive voices in Israeli-Palestinian discourse.

Sunday 17:00

Molly Crabapple

Historian of the Jewish Left — Via Zoom

Her latest book, Here Where We Live Is Our Country: The Story of the Jewish Bund, is a New York Times bestseller — a history of the mass socialist movement that organised millions of Eastern European Jews in the early 20th century and offered a vision of Jewish life rooted in diaspora, solidarity, and struggle rather than nationalism. A timely intervention into debates about what Jewish identity can mean.

Sunday 16:00

Samer Sinijlawi

Palestinian Voice of Reform — Via Zoom

Imprisoned at fifteen during the First Intifada, Sinijlawi is chairman of the Jerusalem Development Fund and a leading independent voice within Palestinian politics. He has challenged both the Palestinian Authority and Hamas, calling for democratic reforms and a pragmatic path to coexistence. A regular and popular speaker at JDI events.

Live Recording — Anthony Fish Hodgson & Caitlin Le Roith

The monthly podcast about Zionism, Jewish identity, and ideology — two young anti-Zionists who don't agree with liberal Zionism. We've invited them to tell us why. Our audience are welcome to argue back. Live-recorded at Cape Town, streamed to Johannesburg.

The Case for Support

Why JDI's Work Matters — and Why It Is Difficult

JDI occupies an uncomfortable position, deliberately. We are Jewish — this is not optional or rhetorical — and we hold our community to account. That combination makes us genuinely unpopular in certain circles, which is exactly the point.

The vacuum we fill

When a South African chief rabbi publicly praises Trump and Musk without acknowledging their neo-fascist affiliations, JDI says so. When the Jewish Board of Deputies attacks protesters outside the Johannesburg Holocaust Centre with language our members consider disproportionate, JDI says so. When Israel's attack on Iran in February 2026 is greeted with enthusiasm in Jewish circles while its recklessness goes unexamined, JDI says so.

There is no other organisation willing to say these things from within the Jewish community. That is not a boast. It is a problem that we are trying to address.

The price of independence

JDI members face social ostracism, professional pressure, and sometimes worse for their public positions. Our founding chair, Anton Harber — who helped build the Weekly Mail during the apartheid years, the newspaper that kept democratic journalism alive when the state was trying to kill it — knows what it costs to be an independent voice. So do our other directors and members.

We do not seek sympathy for this. We note it because it clarifies what it means to support JDI: you are supporting the act of speaking truth in an environment where that carries real consequences.

The moment we are in

As we write, Gaza is entering its third year of devastation. South Africa's ICJ case against Israel has focused global attention on this country as a moral actor in the conflict. The South African Jewish community is under pressure to choose sides — and most institutions have chosen the side of silence or complicity.

The Festival of Dangerous Ideas 2026 responds to this moment directly. By bringing Avrum Burg, Arab Barghouti, Azhar Cachalia, and Standing Together's Sally Abed to South African audiences on Mandela's birthday, we are making a public argument: that peace is possible, that it has precedent, and that the South African example — a negotiated settlement between people who had done terrible things to each other — is not naive idealism but lived, recent history.

Our relationship with the Mauerberger Foundation Fund

When the Mauerberger Foundation Fund came under attack from Jewish communal voices for supporting organisations like ours, JDI published a public statement in the Foundation's defence. We endorsed its 80-year record of social justice funding and called out the attacks as hypocritical and politically motivated.

We did this because it was true and because it was right. The Mauerberger Foundation's willingness to continue funding progressive causes under communal pressure is itself an act of courage that deserves to be named as such. We are proud to be among the organisations you have supported, and we ask you to continue.

"Jews who care about justice cannot remain silent when injustice is perpetrated in their name. Speaking out is not a betrayal of Jewish identity — it is an expression of it."

— JDI Founding Statement, June 2017

Governance & Accountability

Structure, Oversight, and Financial Management

Legal Status

JDI SA is a registered Non-Profit Organisation in South Africa. We comply with all NPO reporting requirements and submit annual financial statements to the Department of Social Development.

All-Volunteer Leadership

Every director on the national committee is a volunteer receiving no remuneration. Donated funds go directly to programme delivery, not to committee-level salaries or overheads.

Democratic Accountability

Directors are elected annually at a General Meeting. Any signed member may vote. Leadership remains accountable to the membership, not to a self-perpetuating board.

Financial Controls

JDI's finances are managed by the Johannesburg committee with national oversight. All significant expenditures require committee approval. Financial reports are presented at each AGM.

Reporting to Funders

We will provide the Mauerberger Foundation Fund with a full narrative and financial report within three months of the Festival, and an annual programme report by December 2026.

Transparency

All JDI statements, event summaries, and governance documents are publicly available at jdisa.org. We do not hold private positions contrary to our public commitments.
