Letter from the Chair

June 2026

The Trustees
Mauerberger Foundation Fund
Cape Town

Dear Trustees,

The Jewish Democratic Initiative was founded nine years ago on a simple but demanding premise: that South African Jews have a responsibility to speak out when injustice is perpetrated in the name of the Jewish people — whether in Gaza, the West Bank, or here in our own community.

In those nine years, we have built something that did not exist before. We are an independent, volunteer-run organisation providing a home for Jews who believe in social justice, democratic pluralism, and a negotiated settlement in Israel-Palestine. Our members include lawyers, academics, artists, doctors and community leaders. Our work spans public statements, educational events, a daily news service (Chadashot), and our annual Festival of Dangerous Ideas.

This year's Festival — our second — takes place on Saturday 18 and Sunday 19 July, at the Hellenic Community Centre in Cape Town and the Killarney Country Club in Johannesburg. It falls on Nelson Mandela's birthday: a deliberate choice, because South Africa's own journey from apartheid to negotiated democracy is the model we hold up for Israel-Palestine.

The speaker line-up is extraordinary. Avrum Burg, former Speaker of the Knesset and interim President of Israel, will be with us in person for both days. Arab Barghouti — son of Marwan Barghouti, the man widely described as the Palestinian Mandela — will meet Azhar Cachalia, a founding member of the Free Mandela Campaign, in what will be one of the most resonant conversations in recent South African civil society history. They are joined by Sally Abed of Standing Together, Rabbi Arik Ascherman, Molly Crabapple, Jonathan Shapiro (Zapiro), Mo Husseini, Samer Sinijlawi, and others.

We are grateful to the Mauerberger Foundation Fund for its previous support, and for the courage it showed in continuing that support when it came under sustained attack from within the Jewish community. We spoke out in public solidarity when that happened — not strategically, but because it was the right thing to do. The relationship between our organisations is grounded in shared values, and we are proud of that.

We present this proposal with confidence. The work is urgent, the speakers are ready, the venues are booked. What we need is your partnership.

Irwin Manoim
Chair, Jewish Democratic Initiative SA
Executive Summary

JDI SA in 2026: An Independent Voice at a Critical Moment

The Jewish Democratic Initiative is South Africa's only independent progressive Jewish organisation — a non-profit founded by community members who believe that the Jewish prophetic tradition demands we speak out for justice, not just for Jews.

As the conflict in Gaza enters its third year, the divisions within Jewish communities worldwide have deepened. South Africa's Jewish community is no exception. The mainstream communal bodies have, in our assessment, failed to provide moral leadership; they have closed ranks around Israeli government policy, silenced dissent, and excluded those who hold different views. JDI exists to fill that void.

About the Organisation

Who We Are and What We Stand For

JDI is an umbrella organisation, not a monolith. Our members hold a range of views about Israel-Palestine, but are united in one core belief: that justice requires an honest, equitable, negotiated settlement in which all parties — Israeli and Palestinian — are represented.

We were founded in June 2017, the 50th anniversary of the Israeli occupation of the West Bank and Gaza, by a group of South African Jews who felt that the mainstream communal bodies no longer represented their values. Our founding was inspired by South Africa's own tradition of Jewish activism — the disproportionate Jewish contribution to the anti-apartheid struggle — and by the recognition that silence in the face of injustice is itself a choice.

We honour that tradition. We seek to re-connect the South African Jewish community to these roots.

JDI operates on a fully volunteer basis. All directors are unpaid. We are funded through member donations, event income, and the support of foundations like the Mauerberger Foundation Fund.

What JDI Does

Our work falls into three overlapping areas:

Public Statements

We publish statements on key events as they unfold — from Israeli policy decisions to South African politics — providing a Jewish progressive perspective in the public record.

Education & Events

Webinars, panel discussions, film screenings, and our annual Festival of Dangerous Ideas — bringing voices to South African audiences that are rarely heard in mainstream communal spaces.

Chadashot Service

A free daily WhatsApp news digest covering Israel-Palestine and South African Jewry — sent to hundreds of subscribers, curated by our editorial team.

Coalition Building

We work alongside other progressive organisations — Jewish, Muslim, Christian, and secular — who share our commitment to peace, democratic values, and universal human rights.

Centrepiece of Our 2026 Programme

The Festival of Dangerous Ideas 2026

Our second Festival brings together the voices you will not hear at your shul, your dinner table, or your corporate gala — over two extraordinary days, simultaneously in Cape Town and Johannesburg.

The Festival takes place on Saturday 18 and Sunday 19 July — Nelson Mandela's birthday. This is a deliberate symbolic choice. JDI's core contention is that South Africa's own negotiated settlement, achieved against overwhelming odds through dialogue between sworn enemies, offers a genuine model for Israel-Palestine. Mandela's birthday is the right occasion to say so.

The underlying theme: How do Jews and Palestinians break free of the spiral of violence and find peace? We have chosen speakers who are neither propagandists nor comfortable — people who have paid a real price for their convictions and who refuse the scripts on offer from either side.

Venues

Cape Town: Hellenic Community Centre, Mouille Point

Johannesburg: Killarney Country Club, Houghton

Format

Most sessions are live-streamed between the two venues. Some sessions are local. A lunch, a dinner, and teas are included. The audience at each venue can interact with speakers and, where configured, with the audience at the other venue.

Booking

Tickets are available via Quicket. Delegates attend at the venue in their city; all sessions are visible at both.

The Speakers

Saturday — 14:15 & Sunday — Closing
Avrum Burg
Conscience of Israel

Once one of Israel's most influential political figures — former Speaker of the Knesset, interim President, chairman of the Jewish Agency — Avrum Burg has spent the last two decades as his country's most searching critic. He has called for Jews worldwide to petition the International Court of Justice over crimes against humanity in Gaza. He will appear in Johannesburg on Saturday and Cape Town on Sunday. His presence in South Africa on Mandela's birthday is itself a statement.

Saturday — 15:30
Arab Barghouti & Azhar Cachalia
Free Barghouti Campaign / Free Mandela Campaign

A session that exists only at JDI. Arab Barghouti — son of Marwan Barghouti, the imprisoned Palestinian leader widely described as "the Palestinian Mandela" — in dialogue with Azhar Cachalia, a founding member of South Africa's Free Mandela Campaign and former judge on the Supreme Court of Appeal. Two campaigns, two freedom fighters, one conversation about what liberation actually requires.

Sunday — 10:00
Sally Abed
Co-Director, Standing Together

Co-director of Standing Together — Israel's largest grassroots movement of Jews and Arabs — which recently formed a political party to contest elections on a platform of partnership rather than division. Abed sits on the Haifa City Council. A Palestinian woman who recognises grief on both sides and insists that liberation and equality for all are not a compromise but the only way forward.

Sunday — 11:30
Rabbi Arik Ascherman
Rabbi for Human Rights

For three decades, Ascherman has practised "protective presence" activism: physically shielding Palestinian farmers from settler violence. He has been stabbed, beaten, and arrested for this work. A recipient of the Gandhi Peace Prize, he insists that Israeli safety and Palestinian rights are not competing demands — they are the same struggle.

Sunday — 09:00
Jonathan Shapiro (Zapiro)
South Africa's Most Celebrated Cartoonist

For three decades the country's most admired political cartoonist — and a pariah to many in his own Jewish community. Zapiro will speak about the silencing of images: how Israel-Palestine has become a "no-go" area for cartoonists at leading newspapers worldwide, as pro-Israel lobbying intimidates editors into censorship or dismissal.

Saturday — 20:00
Mo Husseini
Palestinian Filmmaker & Activist

A Palestinian-American who studied at UC Berkeley and cut his teeth at George Lucas's Industrial Light & Magic. Since October 7th he has emerged as one of the most distinctive voices in the discourse — rejecting Hamas while insisting on Palestinian dignity. His viral "50 Completely True Things" reached hundreds of thousands across the political spectrum. He co-directed The Path Forward alongside Oscar-nominated Julie Cohen.

Sunday — 16:00
Samer Sinijlawi
Palestinian Voice of Reform

Imprisoned at fifteen during the First Intifada, Sinijlawi emerged as a leading independent voice within Palestinian politics. Chairman of the Jerusalem Development Fund, he has challenged both the Palestinian Authority and Hamas, calling for democratic reforms and a pragmatic path to coexistence. A regular and popular speaker at JDI events.

Sunday — 17:00
Molly Crabapple
Historian of the Jewish Left

Her latest book, Here Where We Live Is Our Country: The Story of the Jewish Bund, is a New York Times bestseller — a history of the mass socialist movement that organised millions of Eastern European Jews in the early 20th century and offered a vision of Jewish life rooted in diaspora, solidarity, and struggle rather than nationalism. A timely intervention into debates about what Jewish identity can and should mean.

Saturday — 16:45
Scenes From the Divide
Film & Discussion — Alison Klayman, Director

An intimate documentary short following a polarised Jewish community in the lead-up to the election of New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani. Directed by award-winning filmmaker Alison Klayman, the film steps directly into homes on both sides of the fracture. Screened separately in Cape Town and Johannesburg, each followed by discussion of how its themes resonate in our own community.

Saturday — 18:00 (Cape Town)
The Bad Jews Podcast
Live Recording — Anthony Fish Hodgson & Caitlin Le Roith

The cheekily named monthly podcast about Zionism, Jewish identity, and ideology — two articulate young anti-Zionists who don't agree with liberal Zionism. We've invited them to tell us why. Our audience are welcome to argue back. The session will be live-recorded at our Cape Town venue and streamed to Johannesburg.

Sunday — 14:30
Listening From the Heart
Parents Circle Film — Presented by Jocelyn Horwitz

The Parents Circle – Families Forum unites over 800 bereaved Israeli and Palestinian families who have lost loved ones to the conflict. Believing reconciliation is prerequisite to peace, they focus on dialogue and shared humanity instead of revenge. Presented by Cape Town educator Jocelyn Horwitz.

Sunday — 13:30 (Cape Town)
The Youth Talk Back to Us
Intergenerational Panel

What are the concerns and aspirations of Jewish youth at this divided and troubled time in our community? A panel of young people speak to each other — and to the bobbas and zeiders — about identity, responsibility, and what comes next.

The Case for Support

Why JDI's Work Matters — and Why It Is Difficult

JDI occupies an uncomfortable position, deliberately. We are Jewish — this is not optional or rhetorical — and we hold our community to account. That combination makes us genuinely unpopular in certain circles, which is exactly the point.

The vacuum we fill

When a South African chief rabbi publicly praises Trump and Musk without acknowledging their neo-fascist affiliations, JDI says so. When the Jewish Board of Deputies attacks protesters outside the Johannesburg Holocaust Centre with language described by our members as disproportionate and unwarranted, JDI says so. When the Israel attack on Iran in February 2026 is greeted with enthusiasm in Jewish circles while its recklessness goes unexamined, JDI says so.

There is no other organisation willing to say these things from within the Jewish community. That is not a boast. It is a problem that we are trying to address.

The price of independence

JDI members face social ostracism, professional pressure, and sometimes worse for their public positions. Our founding chair, Anton Harber — who helped build the Weekly Mail during the apartheid years, the newspaper that kept democratic journalism alive when the state was trying to kill it — knows what it costs to be an independent voice. So do our other directors.

We do not seek sympathy for this. We note it because it clarifies what it means to support JDI: you are supporting the act of speaking truth in an environment where that carries real consequences.

The moment we are in

As we write, Gaza is entering its third year of devastation. South Africa's ICJ case against Israel has focused global attention on this country as a moral actor in the conflict. The South African Jewish community is under pressure to choose sides — and most institutions have chosen the side of silence or complicity.

The Festival of Dangerous Ideas 2026 responds to this moment directly. By bringing Avrum Burg, Arab Barghouti, Azhar Cachalia, and Standing Together's Sally Abed to South African audiences on Mandela's birthday, we are making a public argument: that peace is possible, that it has precedent, and that the South African example — negotiated settlement between people who had done terrible things to each other — is not naive idealism but lived history.

The MFF partnership

When the Mauerberger Foundation Fund came under attack from Jewish communal voices for supporting organisations like ours, JDI published a public statement in your defence. We endorsed the Foundation's 80-year record of social justice funding and called out the attacks as hypocritical and politically motivated.

We did this because it was true and because it was right. The Mauerberger Foundation's willingness to continue funding progressive causes under communal pressure is itself an act of courage that deserves to be named as such. We are proud to be among the organisations you have supported, and we ask you to continue.

"Jews who care about justice cannot remain silent when injustice is perpetrated in their name. Speaking out is not a betrayal of Jewish identity — it is an expression of it."

JDI Founding Statement, June 2017

Governance & Accountability

Structure, Oversight, and Financial Management

Legal Status

JDI SA is a registered Non-Profit Organisation in South Africa. We are compliant with all NPO reporting requirements and submit annual financial statements to the Department of Social Development.

All-Volunteer Leadership

Every director on the national committee is a volunteer who receives no remuneration. This means that donated funds go directly to programme delivery, not to administrative overheads or salaries at committee level.

Democratic Accountability

Directors are elected annually at a General Meeting. Any signed member may vote. This structure ensures that leadership remains genuinely accountable to the membership, not to a self-perpetuating board.

Financial Controls

JDI's finances are managed by the Johannesburg committee, with oversight from the national committee. All significant expenditures require committee approval. Financial reports are presented at each AGM.

Reporting to Funders

We are committed to providing the Mauerberger Foundation Fund with a full narrative and financial report within three months of the Festival, and an annual report on programme activities by December 2026.

Transparency

All JDI statements, event summaries, and governance documents are publicly available at jdisa.org. We do not hold private positions or act privately contrary to our public commitments.

National Committee 2026

The committee combines editorial experience (Manoim, Harber), legal and human rights expertise (Price), community leadership (Dubb, Schkolne, Frankental, Miller), and strategic management (Lubinsky, G. Harber). All bring established professional reputations and stand behind JDI's work publicly.
